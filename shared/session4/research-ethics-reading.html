Where we start

Every tool carries a politics.

To use a tool is to become entangled in who made it, who benefits, and who pays. Artificial intelligence is not an exception to the history of tools. It is a new case within that history.

LoomMechanized labor and reorganized who controlled production.
↓
Printing pressChanged who could publish, distribute, and authorize knowledge.
↓
SmartphoneChanged who could connect, observe, record, and participate.
↓
Large language model toolsChange who can generate, synthesize, classify, and present knowledge.
Core claim: understanding the material and institutional system beneath AI is the foundation for accountable research.
Movement One

Understanding the AI ecosystem

When researchers say “I used AI,” the ethical object is rarely a single model. It is an ecosystem.

OrganizationCompany, laboratory, university, state, or consortium.
InfrastructureCompute, networks, platforms, data centers, electricity, and water.
InterfaceThe environment through which users encounter the system.
HarnessPrompts, tools, memory, retrieval, moderation, and workflow logic.
ModelThe trained statistical system.
DataThe material from which the system learns or retrieves.

Three material realities behind every prompt

People

  • Research and engineering
  • Annotation and evaluation
  • Content moderation
  • Maintenance and support

Resources

  • Collected, licensed, inferred, or appropriated data
  • Electricity and water
  • Chips, minerals, data centers, and waste

Institutions

  • Capital investment
  • Governance and contracts
  • Law, standards, and procurement
  • Organizations controlling access

Why these tools are made

Motives overlap: achievement, public benefit, profit, competition, prestige, coordination, and control. Ethical analysis becomes necessary precisely because these motives cannot be cleanly separated.

Awareness is a spectrum

1. Entry awarenessBasic orientation through reliable introductory sources.
2. Serious introductory readingAt least one sustained scholarly account of AI’s social and material conditions.
3. Current literatureEngagement with contemporary research in the relevant field.
4. Informed judgmentThe ability to connect technical choices to ethical and institutional consequences.

Access is not permission

Accessibility

Can I obtain the material?

Permission

Do I have the legal right to use it?

Consent

Did affected people meaningfully agree?

Legitimacy

Is this use appropriate in this relationship and context?

Cameroon follows droit d’auteur, with statutory copyright exceptions rather than a U.S.-style open-ended fair-use test. Technical accessibility therefore does not itself establish permission.

Where you stand matters

AI access is layered on top of uneven connectivity. Graduate researchers using advanced AI systems in Cameroon are not merely users; they are early norm-setters whose practices may influence institutions, disciplines, and later cohorts.

Movement Two

Making research accountable

Accountability is not a checklist of commandments. It is a set of practices that make judgment inspectable.

Practice 1

Document what you did

Prompting, coding, retrieval, editing, comparison, and synthesis are different activities. Name the activity and preserve the workflow.

  • Prompts and major iterations
  • Models, tools, harnesses, and interfaces
  • Materials ingested for retrieval or analysis
  • Points where human judgment changed the output
  • What the model claimed and what you independently verified
Practice 2

Cite so another person can check you

A citation is provenance infrastructure: a path back to evidence.

  • Prefer DOI, ISBN, URI, and stable identifiers over bare URLs
  • Maintain full bibliographic metadata
  • Keep Markdown source, not only PDF output
  • Preserve conversion provenance when transforming PDFs
  • Open cited sources and verify quotations and claims
Practice 3

Disclose the process, not merely the tool

“I used ChatGPT” is rarely sufficient. A useful disclosure explains what the system did, what material it received, how outputs were checked, and what remains the researcher’s responsibility.

Practical recommendation: maintain one disclosure.md file for each paper or project.

Practice 4

Share so the work can be improved

Reproducibility is a form of generosity. Preserve conversations, files, bibliographic records, transformations, and enough workflow detail for another person or model to reproduce, critique, or extend the work.

Movement Three

Responsibility and judgment

Ethical research is not only what you can defend privately. It is what you can defend across contexts.

ResearcherWhat cognition do you offload, and why?
InstitutionWhat policies govern your research, data, and tools?
ProfessionWhat do disciplinary codes and norms require?
SocietyWho bears costs, who gains capacity, and who is excluded?

Early adopters set the norms

It does not matter only how you arrived at early access—through merit, geography, timing, family, or institution. What matters is that your fluency will shape what other people understand responsible AI research to look like.

Someone teaches first. If you are among the first, that someone is you.

Direction, not abstinence

Research always spends something: energy, labor, attention, opportunity, and institutional trust. The ethical question is not whether a cost exists. It is whether what stands at the end was worth the cost and whether that judgment can be defended publicly.

Point the compute somewhere worth the joules.
Before you go

Three questions to carry forward

Awareness

Where are you, honestly, on the awareness spectrum?

Documentation

Which part of your AI process is least documented?

Norm-setting

Who is one person you could teach this framework to in the next month?

Research ethics begins with understanding. It becomes real through accountable practice.
