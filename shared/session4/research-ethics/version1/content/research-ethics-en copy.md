# Requirements for doing ethical research with LLMs

## Understanding tools, and how and why they are made

### What are LLM tools?
- Model — the trained statistical system
- Data — the material from which the model learns
- Harness — the software that structures prompts, tools, memory, and outputs
- Interface — the environment through which users encounter the system
- Infrastructure — the compute, networks, platforms, and institutions that make it available
- Organization — the company, laboratory, government, or community that controls its development and use

### How are these tools made?
- Labor — visible and hidden human work, including research, annotation, moderation, maintenance, and extraction
- Data — collected, licensed, purchased, donated, inferred, or appropriated
- Energy and materials — electricity, water, chips, minerals, data centers, and waste
- Capital — concentrated investment that shapes what can be built and by whom
- Governance — laws, contracts, standards, and institutional decisions that authorize development

### Why are these tools made?
- Knowledge and achievement — to expand scientific, creative, administrative, or technical capacity
- Profit and competition — to capture markets, reduce costs, and secure strategic advantage
- Control and coordination — to classify, predict, manage, monitor, or influence people and systems
- Public benefit — to improve access, safety, education, health, or institutional capacity
- Prestige and power — to establish authority, legitimacy, or geopolitical leadership

## Making processes and procedures explicit

### Activities: what we do

- Prompting vs. code — what are we doing, exactly? Describe it (or have a model describe it), and document it.
- Many methods: one-shot, zero-shot, multi-model, agentic — across harness and interface.
- If ingesting for RAG: record provenance and process.

### Citing: citations and provenance

- DOI / ISBN / URI (not URL) for every citation; Markdown, not PDF.
- Full metadata to the BibTeX standard — Zotero and other reference managers read it, and so do models (read and write).
- Verify: open the cited sources, confirm the quotations exist and support the claim.
- If ingesting: preserve the PDF-to-Markdown provenance.

### Disclosing: disclosure practices

- The workbench: keep the full conversations — you keep it all.
- Adopt a standard practice, and use a model to manage the repository.
- Generate a disclosure note (.md) for each paper.

### Sharing: reproducibility

- The goal is for another person or model to reproduce or improve on your work.
- Reproducibility supports validation, verification, and evaluation.


## Making choices and weighing tradeoffs that honor values and obligations

### Personal

- How much cognition we offload is a personal choice.
- Track what you offload, and be sure it is a wise choice.

### Institutions

- Universities have academic-integrity codes that govern how you use these tools.
- They also have rules about ingesting data that affect how you use them.

### Professions

- As engineers, computer scientists, or scientists, we are bound by professional codes of ethics that guide our conduct.

### Society

- At the top of the diffusion curve, we expect these students to share and share alike,
- and to identify serious biases so they can be corrected — or at least so others are aware of potential challenges to society.
- Energy is never free — the question is never whether you spend it, but whether what stands at the end was worth it.
- Point the compute somewhere worth the joules, toward people beyond yourself.

### Accessibility is technical. Permission is legal. Consent is relational. Legitimacy is contextual.

* **Accessibility** asks: *Can I get the material?*

* **Permission** asks: *Do I have the legal right to use it?*

* **Consent** asks: *Did the people affected meaningfully agree?*

* **Legitimacy** asks: *Is this use appropriate in this social, cultural, institutional, and ethical context?*

* Cameroon follows ***droit d'auteur*** (author's rights), with a **closed list of copyright exceptions** (Law No. 2000/011 of 19 December 2000, as amended in 2008; OAPI/Bangui Agreement; Berne Convention; TRIPS).

* **Implication:** If your intended use is **not** covered by a statutory exception or a license, the fact that material is technically accessible does **not** make its use lawful or ethical.
